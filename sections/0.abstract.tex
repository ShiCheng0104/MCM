\begin{abstract}

To demystify the "black box" of audience voting in \textit{Dancing with the Stars} (DWTS), we develop a comprehensive mathematical framework addressing a fundamental \textbf{constrained inverse problem}: reconstructing unobservable fan votes from judge scores and elimination outcomes across 34 seasons.

\textbf{Two-Layer Bayesian Framework.} We tackle the information bottleneck by constructing informative priors through a calibrated power-law model ($V_i \propto S_i^{1.2}$) fusing judge scores, celebrity features, and Google Trends data, then employing constrained optimization (SLSQP algorithm) to ensure estimates match observed eliminations. This achieves \textbf{91.7\% prediction accuracy} overall and \textbf{99.5\% for the percentage method}, with median uncertainty \textbf{CV = 0.117}.

\textbf{Mechanism Analysis.} GBDT models with SHAP decomposition reveal divergent evaluation standards: partner experience contributes 18.2\% to judge scores but only 7.4\% to fan votes (judges prioritize technique), while celebrity industry drives 23.6\% of fan variance yet 9.1\% of judge scores (audiences favor charisma). The rank method amplifies fan influence by 12.8\% relative to percentage method in tied scenarios.

\textbf{The "Dramatic Arc" Voting System.} Our phase-adaptive design dynamically adjusts judge-fan weights (55-40-60\% judge across early/mid/late stages) and introduces a controversy bonus mechanism (5\% bonus for judge-fan ranking divergence $\geq 3$). Historical simulation validates \textbf{综合得分 0.860 (ranked \#1)}, projecting 47\% social media growth while maintaining an optimal 11.1\% controversy rate. Monte Carlo testing confirms 89.3\% accuracy retention under perturbations.

Our work delivers a \textbf{production-ready voting protocol} demonstrating how rigorous modeling reconciles entertainment imperatives with competitive integrity, offering quantified commercial value for reality television producers.
